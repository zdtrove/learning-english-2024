TRAFFIC CONGESTION IN HO CHI MINH CITY — CAUSES & SOLUTIONS

Cause 1:

Too many private vehicles, especially motorcycles. For example, millions of people use motorcycles every day because they’re cheap, convenient, and easy to get around on.

Solution 1:

Improve public transportation, for example, expand the metro and bus networks and make buses more frequent and reliable.

Solution 2:

Encourage people to use public transportation, for example, provide park-and-ride facilities near metro stations so people can leave their motorcycles outside crowded areas.

Solution 3:

Limit private vehicles in crowded areas, for example, limit the number of motorcycles or cars entering the city center during rush hours.

Cause 2:

Public transportation isn’t convenient enough, so many people prefer using their own motorcycles. For example, someone might have to take one bus, then another bus, and then walk quite a long way to get to work.

Solution 1:

Expand the public transportation network, for example, build more metro lines and bus routes to connect residential areas with business districts.

Solution 2:

Make it easier to switch between different types of transportation, for example, make it easy to transfer from a bus to a metro without having to walk a long way.

Solution 3:

Improve the quality of public transportation, for example, provide cleaner vehicles, shorter waiting times, real-time schedules, and easier payment methods.

Cause 3:

Rapid population growth, because Ho Chi Minh City attracts people from many other provinces for jobs and education. For example, many people move to the city because they think there are more job opportunities there.

Solution 1:

Develop satellite cities, for example, develop areas outside the city center with enough housing, schools, hospitals, and shopping centers.

Solution 2:

Improve transportation between the city and surrounding areas, for example, build metro lines or regional railways connecting suburban areas to the city.

Solution 3:

Encourage businesses to move to suburban areas, for example, give companies incentives to open offices or factories outside the city center.

Cause 4:

Jobs are concentrated in the city center, while many people live in the suburbs, so they have to travel long distances to work every day. For example, someone might live in Binh Tan but work in District 1, so they have to travel across the city every morning.

Solution 1:

Develop more business centers, for example, create more job opportunities in areas outside the traditional city center.

Solution 2:

Build schools, hospitals, and shopping centers near residential areas, so people don’t have to travel to another district just to go shopping or see a doctor.

Solution 3:

Encourage remote or hybrid work, for example, office workers could work from home one or two days a week.

Cause 5:

The roads can’t keep up with the growing number of vehicles, so they become overloaded during rush hours. For example, a road that was built for much less traffic may now have to handle thousands of motorcycles and cars every hour.

Solution 1:

Build more roads and bridges, for example, construct new roads, overpasses, and bridges to give traffic more space.

Solution 2:

Widen important roads, for example, widen roads that are often congested if there’s enough space to do so.

Solution 3:

Build better road networks, for example, connect major roads with ring roads and highways so traffic doesn’t have to go through the city center.

Cause 6:

Poor traffic management, such as inefficient traffic lights, badly designed intersections, and vehicles stopping in the wrong places. For example, an intersection can become heavily congested if the traffic light gives the same amount of green time to roads with very different levels of traffic.

Solution 1:

Use smart traffic lights, for example, traffic lights could automatically change their timing based on real-time traffic conditions.

Solution 2:

Use cameras and AI to monitor traffic, for example, cameras could detect accidents, illegal parking, or unusually heavy traffic.

Solution 3:

Improve the design of intersections, for example, redesign dangerous or overloaded intersections to help traffic move more smoothly.

Cause 7:

Poor driving behavior, such as running red lights, suddenly changing lanes, driving on sidewalks, or blocking intersections. For example, one driver stopping in the middle of an intersection can stop traffic from moving in several directions.

Solution 1:

Increase traffic enforcement, for example, give higher fines for serious traffic violations.

Solution 2:

Install more traffic cameras, for example, automatically record vehicles that run red lights or use the wrong lane.

Solution 3:

Improve traffic education, for example, teach children and young people about traffic safety and responsible driving from an early age.

Illegal parking and people using sidewalks for other purposes, which reduces the space available for both vehicles and pedestrians. For example, cars or motorcycles parked on the side of a narrow road can make an already crowded street even narrower.

Solution 1:

Build more parking facilities, for example, construct multi-story parking buildings in busy areas.

Solution 2:

Strictly enforce parking rules, for example, fine or tow vehicles that block traffic lanes or sidewalks.

Solution 3:

Improve sidewalks, for example, clearly separate pedestrian areas from parking areas and keep sidewalks free for pedestrians.

Cause 9:

Construction projects, because road and infrastructure work often reduces the number of available lanes. For example, building a metro line or drainage system may close one or two lanes for several months.

Solution 1:

Improve construction planning, for example, avoid carrying out several major projects in the same area at the same time.

Solution 2:

Do some construction work at night, for example, schedule road work when traffic is lighter.

Solution 3:

Provide alternative routes, for example, clearly inform drivers about other routes before closing a major road.

Cause 10:

Traffic accidents, because even a small accident can block one or more lanes and cause a long traffic jam. For example, if a motorcycle accident happens on a narrow road during rush hour, traffic behind it can quickly build up.

Solution 1:

Improve emergency response, for example, send traffic police or emergency teams to accident locations as quickly as possible.

Solution 2:

Move damaged vehicles quickly, for example, use tow trucks to remove vehicles that are blocking traffic.

Solution 3:

Improve road safety, for example, install better lighting, road signs, lane markings, and safety barriers.

Cause 11:

Heavy rain and flooding, which can slow traffic down or make some roads difficult to use. For example, motorcycles and cars have to drive much more slowly when a road is badly flooded.

Solution 1:

Improve the drainage system, for example, upgrade drainage pipes and pumping stations in areas that often get flooded.

Solution 2:

Build better flood-control systems, for example, improve canals, reservoirs, and other systems that can help manage rainwater.

Solution 3:

Provide real-time traffic information, for example, warn drivers about flooded roads through navigation apps and traffic information systems.

Cause 12:

Too many trucks and commercial vehicles, especially around ports, industrial areas, and logistics centers. For example, container trucks can take up a lot of road space and slow down traffic on major roads.

Solution 1:

Limit trucks during rush hours, for example, prevent large trucks from entering busy areas during the morning and evening rush hours.

Solution 2:

Create dedicated routes for trucks, for example, direct trucks to specific roads that are designed to handle heavy vehicles.

Solution 3:

Move logistics centers away from densely populated areas, for example, build large warehouses and distribution centers in suburban or industrial areas.

Cause 13:

Peak-hour commuting, because too many people travel at almost exactly the same time. For example, thousands of workers and students may leave home between 7:00 and 8:00 a.m. and return home between 5:00 and 7:00 p.m.

Solution 1:

Introduce flexible working hours, for example, some employees could work from 7:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., while others could work from 9:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m.

Solution 2:

Promote hybrid work, for example, office workers could work from home one or two days a week.

Solution 3:

Stagger school and work schedules, for example, schools and companies could start at different times instead of everyone starting at 7:30 or 8:00 a.m.

Cause 14:

Too many ride-hailing and delivery vehicles, because services like food delivery and ride-hailing create extra trips on the roads. For example, a delivery rider may have to travel through several streets just to deliver one meal.

Solution 1:

Create delivery hubs, for example, riders could collect multiple orders from a central location instead of traveling long distances.

Solution 2:

Encourage batch delivery, for example, one rider could deliver several orders to customers in the same neighborhood.

Solution 3:

Create designated pickup and drop-off areas, for example, apartment buildings and shopping malls could have special areas for ride-hailing and delivery vehicles.

Cause 15:

Lack of parking, which can cause drivers to spend a lot of time looking for a parking space. For example, a driver may drive around several blocks before finding a place to park, which can slow down traffic.

Solution 1:

Build more multi-story parking facilities, for example, construct parking buildings near shopping areas and office districts.

Solution 2:

Develop park-and-ride facilities, for example, people could park their motorcycles or cars near a metro station and continue their journey by public transportation.

Solution 3:

Use flexible parking fees, for example, charge higher parking fees in crowded downtown areas to discourage people from driving there.
