QUESTION & SAMPLE MODEL ANSWER IELTS Speaking Part 3

1. Why do people feel nervous when they are giving a speech to others?

I think public speaking is one of the biggest challenges for many people, especially those who don't have much experience with it. Like any other interpersonal skill, being able to give a clear and interesting speech to a group of people takes practice.

For me, there are two main reasons why people feel nervous. The first one is the fear of how the audience will react. People naturally care about what others think of them, so presenters may worry about making mistakes, forgetting something, or being judged by the audience. Because of that, they sometimes put too much pressure on themselves to make every part of their speech perfect.

The second reason is the topic itself. If someone has to talk about something they're not familiar with, or something that's too complicated for the audience, they may feel less confident.

So, I think a lack of experience, fear of being judged, and not being familiar with the topic are probably the main reasons people get nervous when giving a speech.

2. How can people improve their public speaking skills?

Thanks to the Internet, there are now plenty of ways to improve your public speaking skills. You can find useful information, courses, and communities online without having to spend a lot of money.

For example, people can take an online course or join an online community where others have the same goal. They can share their experiences, give each other feedback, and learn from people who are already good at public speaking. I think this is quite effective and convenient, and in many cases, it's even free.

If someone wants something more structured, they could attend a workshop or a conference and learn directly from public-speaking experts. They can pick up useful techniques and get advice on things like body language, pronunciation, and how to organize a speech.

But honestly, I think the most important thing is simply to practice. You can watch hundreds of videos about public speaking, but unless you actually stand up and speak in front of other people, it's difficult to make real progress.

So, I'd say learning is important, but regular practice is probably the key.

3. Can you suggest any methods that would help reduce nervousness?

I think everyone experiences nervousness at some point in their lives. It could happen before a job interview, a big presentation, or even when you're meeting your future in-laws for the first time.

Actually, I don't think being nervous is necessarily a bad thing. It usually means that you care about what you're doing and want to do well. The problem is when the nervousness becomes so strong that it affects your performance.

The first thing I'd recommend is taking a few deep breaths. It's hard to perform at your best when you're breathing too quickly or feeling physically tense. Taking a moment to slow down and breathe can help you calm yourself.

Second, don't try to convince yourself that you're not nervous. I think it's better to simply accept the feeling. You can tell yourself, "Yeah, I'm nervous, and that's okay." Once you accept it, you don't have to waste energy trying to fight the feeling.

Finally, I think positive self-talk can be really helpful. Encourage yourself and remind yourself why you're there in the first place. For example, you can tell yourself, "I prepared for this, and I have a reason to be here."

So, for me, the key is to accept the nervousness rather than fight it, calm yourself down physically, and remind yourself that you can handle the situation.
