HOBBIES

→ My favorite hobbies and interests

When it comes to my hobbies, I would say that I have a few different interests rather than just one particular hobby. 
One of my favorite things to do is playing badminton because it allows me to stay active while also having fun. 
I usually play with my friends or colleagues, and I really enjoy the competitive but friendly atmosphere. 
Apart from sports, I also enjoy listening to English podcasts and watching English-speaking videos because I’m trying to improve my listening and speaking skills. 
Sometimes, I also like playing games when I have some free time. 
I wouldn't say that I play games all day, but they are a good way to take my mind off work. 
So overall, my hobbies are a mixture of physical activities, entertainment, and things that help me improve myself. 
I think having different hobbies is important because it gives me more ways to enjoy my free time.

→ When I usually do them

The time I spend on my hobbies depends quite a lot on my work schedule. 
Since I work from Monday to Friday, my weekdays are usually quite busy, so I don't have a lot of free time during the day. 
After work, I normally have dinner, take a short break, and then spend some time on my personal interests. 
For example, I might listen to English content while commuting because that doesn't require me to set aside extra time. 
In the evening, if I'm not too tired, I might practice speaking English, play a game, or watch something for entertainment. 
Weekends are different because I have much more flexibility. 
I usually have more time for outdoor activities, especially badminton. 
I prefer doing physical activities in the morning because the weather is usually more comfortable and I feel more energetic at that time. 
So basically, I try to fit my hobbies around my daily routine instead of treating them as something separate from my life.

→ How often I enjoy them

I enjoy my hobbies on a regular basis, but the frequency depends on what kind of hobby I'm talking about. 
For example, I try to play badminton at least once a week because it's one of the activities I enjoy the most. 
I don't necessarily need to play every day, but having a regular session gives me something to look forward to during the week. 
When it comes to English, I actually spend time on it almost every day because I'm trying to make steady progress. 
I might listen to English in the morning, practice speaking in the evening, or watch English videos when I have some free time. 
As for gaming and other forms of entertainment, I usually do them less frequently because they aren't really a priority for me. 
I think the key is finding a balance. 
I don't want my hobbies to take up all of my free time, but I also don't want to completely give them up because they make my life more enjoyable.

→ Who I usually do them with

Some of my hobbies are things I enjoy doing by myself, while others are much more enjoyable when I have someone to do them with. 
For example, I prefer playing badminton with friends or colleagues because the social aspect makes the activity much more fun. 
We can talk, joke around, encourage each other, and sometimes even have a little friendly competition. 
I think that's one of the reasons why I keep coming back to it. 
On the other hand, activities like listening to podcasts, studying English, or watching videos are things I usually do alone. 
I actually enjoy having some quiet time because I can completely focus on what I'm doing without being interrupted. 
Sometimes I also think that hobbies can help strengthen relationships. 
If you regularly do an activity with someone, you naturally spend more time together and get to know each other better. 
So for me, it really depends on the activity. 
Some hobbies help me socialize, while others give me some valuable time for myself.

→ How I became interested in them

I became interested in my hobbies at different stages of my life, and there wasn't really one specific moment when I decided to have them. 
For example, I became interested in badminton because it was an easy sport to pick up and I could play it with people around me. 
At first, I just played casually for fun, but after playing several times, I started to enjoy it more and became more interested in improving my skills. 
My interest in English developed in a different way. 
Because I work in IT and sometimes communicate with people from other countries, I gradually realized that English could be very useful for both my career and my personal life. 
At some point, I stopped thinking of English as just a school subject and started seeing it as a practical skill. 
That changed the way I learned it. 
I began listening to podcasts, watching videos, and practicing speaking more regularly. 
So I think many of my hobbies started naturally rather than being something I deliberately planned from the beginning.

→ Why I enjoy doing them

There are several reasons why I enjoy my hobbies. 
First of all, they give me a break from my normal routine. 
Work can sometimes be repetitive or mentally demanding, so doing something completely different helps me reset my mind. 
I also enjoy hobbies that give me a sense of progress. 
For example, when I practice English and notice that I can understand something more easily or express myself more naturally, it makes me feel like my effort is paying off. 
The same thing happens with badminton. 
When I improve a particular technique or play better than I did before, I feel a small sense of achievement. 
Another reason is that hobbies give me something to look forward to. 
Even if I've had a stressful week, I know that I'll have some free time to do something I enjoy. 
I think that's really important because life shouldn't be only about work and responsibilities. 
Having activities that genuinely make you happy can make your daily life feel more balanced and enjoyable.

→ How my hobbies help me relax

My hobbies are one of the main ways I deal with stress after a long day at work. 
When I've been sitting in front of a computer for several hours, doing something physical like badminton helps me completely change my state of mind. 
Instead of thinking about work, deadlines, or technical problems, I have to focus on the game and react to what's happening in front of me. 
That helps me forget about work for a while. 
Even hobbies that are less physical can help me relax. 
For example, listening to something interesting or playing a game allows me to switch my attention to something enjoyable. 
Sometimes I don't even need to do anything particularly exciting. 
I can simply sit down, put on my headphones, and listen to something while relaxing. 
I think the important thing is that hobbies give my brain a chance to step away from work. 
They create a clear separation between my professional life and my personal time, which helps me recharge and come back to work with a fresher mind.

→ What I can learn from them

I think hobbies can teach us much more than we initially expect. 
For example, playing badminton has taught me about patience, concentration, and consistency. 
You can't become good at a sport just by playing once or twice. 
You have to practice regularly, make mistakes, and gradually improve. 
That experience has helped me understand that progress usually takes time. 
Learning English has taught me something similar. 
Sometimes I study for weeks without feeling that I'm making much progress, but when I look back after a few months, I can clearly see the difference. 
My hobbies have also taught me how important it is to enjoy the process rather than only focusing on the final result. 
If I only cared about becoming better, I might get frustrated easily. 
Instead, I try to enjoy what I'm doing and let improvement happen naturally. 
So in a way, hobbies can teach us valuable life skills such as discipline, patience, time management, and the ability to keep going even when progress is slow.

→ My most memorable hobby experience

One of my most memorable experiences related to my hobbies was playing badminton with a group of friends and colleagues on a weekend. 
What made it memorable wasn't necessarily the game itself, but the atmosphere around it. 
We weren't playing professionally or anything like that, but everyone was really enthusiastic and wanted to do their best. 
There were moments when we made ridiculous mistakes and laughed about them, and there were also some surprisingly intense rallies where everyone became really competitive. 
After playing for a while, we were exhausted but felt really satisfied. 
We went somewhere afterward, had something to eat, and talked about all kinds of things. 
Looking back, I think that's what makes hobbies special. 
Sometimes the most memorable experiences aren't the ones where you achieve something impressive. 
They're simply moments when you spend time with people you enjoy, have fun, and forget about your everyday responsibilities for a few hours. 
That's the kind of experience I would like to have more often.

→ How much time they require

The amount of time my hobbies require varies quite a lot. 
Some hobbies can be done in just a few minutes, while others require me to set aside a couple of hours. 
For example, I can listen to an English podcast during my commute without having to spend any additional time on it. 
That's quite convenient because I can combine learning with something I already have to do. 
Playing badminton, on the other hand, requires more planning. 
I usually need to spend around one or two hours for a session, including the time it takes to travel there and get ready. 
I try not to let my hobbies take over my schedule, especially on weekdays. 
I have work, household responsibilities, and other things to take care of, so I need to manage my time carefully. 
At the same time, I don't think hobbies should be something we squeeze into the tiny amount of time left after everything else. 
I think it's worth deliberately making time for activities that are important to us.

→ How much money they cost

Another interesting thing about hobbies is how much money they can cost. 
Fortunately, most of my hobbies aren't particularly expensive. 
For example, listening to podcasts or practicing English can be almost free, especially because there are so many free resources available online. 
Playing badminton costs a little more because I need equipment and sometimes have to pay for a court. 
However, compared with some other hobbies, I still think it's relatively affordable. 
I don't believe that an expensive hobby is necessarily a better hobby. 
In fact, some of the most enjoyable activities require very little money. 
You can go for a walk, read a book, listen to music, exercise, or simply spend time with friends without spending much at all. 
Of course, some people enjoy hobbies like photography, traveling, collecting things, or playing musical instruments, which can become quite expensive. 
Personally, I prefer hobbies that give me a lot of enjoyment without putting too much pressure on my budget. 
I think the value of a hobby should be measured by how much happiness or benefit it brings, not simply by how much money we spend on it.

→ Indoor hobbies versus outdoor hobbies

I enjoy both indoor and outdoor hobbies, but they give me completely different experiences. 
Indoor hobbies are usually more convenient because I can do them at home regardless of the weather. 
For example, I can watch a movie, listen to a podcast, practice English, play a game, or read something whenever I have some free time. 
I don't need to travel anywhere, so they're perfect when I'm tired or simply want to stay at home. 
Outdoor hobbies, on the other hand, require more effort, but they also give me the chance to be physically active and get some fresh air. 
Playing badminton is a good example. 
After spending most of my working day indoors and in front of a computer, I actually appreciate having an activity that gets me moving. 
If I had to choose between the two, I wouldn't want to give either one up. 
I think the best approach is to have a balance. 
Indoor hobbies are great for relaxation and convenience, while outdoor hobbies are better when I want to exercise, socialize, and get away from my usual environment.

→ My hobbies during weekdays

During weekdays, I usually choose hobbies that are easy to fit into a busy schedule. 
Since I have to work during the day, I don't have enough time for activities that require several hours. 
One of the things I often do is listen to English during my commute. 
This is particularly convenient because I can turn otherwise unused time into learning time. 
After I get home from work, I usually take some time to rest and have dinner. 
If I still have enough energy, I might spend some time practicing speaking English, watching something interesting, or playing a game. 
I try to avoid doing anything too demanding late at night because I don't want my hobbies to affect my sleep. 
On weekdays, I think the main purpose of my hobbies is to help me relax and maintain a good balance. 
I don't necessarily need to accomplish something every evening. 
Sometimes simply doing something enjoyable for thirty minutes is enough. 
The important thing is to have some personal time after spending most of the day working.

→ My hobbies during the weekend

My weekends are when I have the most freedom to enjoy my hobbies. 
Since I don't have to follow my normal work schedule, I can spend more time on activities that require a little more preparation. 
One thing I particularly enjoy doing on weekends is playing badminton, usually in the morning. 
I prefer the morning because I feel more energetic and the weather is generally more comfortable than later in the day. 
After that, I might have breakfast or coffee with friends or colleagues and spend some time chatting. 
The rest of the weekend is usually more flexible. 
I might stay at home, watch something, play games, practice English, or simply relax. 
I also try not to make every weekend extremely busy. 
Sometimes I deliberately leave a few hours completely free because I think having nothing planned can also be relaxing. 
For me, a good weekend isn't necessarily one where I do lots of exciting things. 
It's one where I have enough time to do what I want, spend time with people I care about, and recharge before another working week begins.

→ Hobbies I want to try someday

There are also several hobbies I'd like to try someday. 
One of them is learning to play a musical instrument, perhaps the guitar. 
I've always thought it would be really interesting to be able to pick up a guitar and play a song instead of just listening to music. 
Another hobby I'd like to explore is photography. 
I think photography is a great way to pay more attention to the world around you because you start noticing small details, interesting places, and beautiful moments that you might normally ignore. 
I'd also like to travel more and perhaps turn traveling into a more regular hobby rather than something I only do occasionally. 
The reason I haven't started some of these hobbies yet is simply that I don't have unlimited time. 
I already have work, exercise, English practice, and other responsibilities. 
However, I don't think I need to start everything at once. 
I can gradually try new things whenever I have more time. 
I believe trying new hobbies is a good way to keep life interesting and discover new sides of yourself.

SPORT

→ My favorite sport and activities

When it comes to sports, badminton is probably my favorite activity. 
I like it because it is relatively easy to get started, but at the same time, there is always room to improve. 
You don't have to be a professional athlete to enjoy it. 
You can simply play casually with friends, have some fun, and still get a pretty good workout. 
I also enjoy the fact that badminton requires a combination of speed, coordination, concentration, and stamina. 
Apart from badminton, I sometimes do simple exercises at home, such as push-ups, squats, burpees, or other bodyweight exercises. 
I don't necessarily follow a strict workout program every day. 
Instead, I try to stay active in ways that are realistic for my lifestyle. 
For me, sports aren't just about building muscles or improving physical performance. 
They're also a way to have fun, relieve stress, and take a break from sitting in front of a computer. 
That's why badminton and simple home exercises are the activities I enjoy the most.

→ How often I play or exercise

I try to exercise regularly rather than only working out when I feel like it. 
In general, I aim to do some kind of physical activity several times a week. 
Badminton is usually something I do about once a week because it requires more time and planning. 
On other days, I might do shorter exercises at home, especially when I don't have enough time to go outside. 
I don't think every workout has to be intense. 
Sometimes even twenty or thirty minutes of exercise is better than doing nothing at all. 
Of course, there are weeks when I'm busy or tired and I don't exercise as much as I would like to. 
I think that's completely normal. 
The important thing for me is to get back into my routine instead of giving up completely. 
I've gradually learned that consistency is more important than trying to exercise extremely hard for a short period of time. 
I'd rather have a realistic routine that I can maintain for years than an ambitious workout plan that I follow for only a few weeks.

→ When I usually exercise

The time when I exercise depends on the type of activity I'm doing. 
For badminton, I usually prefer playing in the morning, especially on weekends. 
I like exercising in the morning because I haven't spent the whole day working yet, so I still have plenty of energy. 
It's also a nice way to start the day because I feel more awake and refreshed afterward. 
On weekdays, however, my schedule is more limited because I have to work during the day. 
If I want to exercise on a weekday, I usually do it in the evening after work. 
Sometimes I don't have enough energy for a long workout, so I just do a short session at home. 
I've found that the best time to exercise isn't necessarily the time that is scientifically perfect; it's the time that I can consistently fit into my schedule. 
If I choose a time that's inconvenient, I'm much more likely to skip the workout. 
So I try to choose realistic times that work naturally with my daily routine.

→ Who I usually play with

I usually play badminton with friends or colleagues rather than playing by myself. 
I think badminton is much more enjoyable when there is someone on the other side of the court. 
We can play against each other, practice different techniques, and sometimes have friendly competitions. 
What I particularly like is the social atmosphere. 
We're not there to become professional athletes, so we can take the game seriously without taking it too seriously. 
We might compete for a few points and then laugh about a funny mistake a few minutes later. 
Playing with other people also motivates me to exercise. 
If I make plans with someone in advance, I'm less likely to cancel because I know the other person is expecting me. 
When I exercise alone, it's much easier to say, "I'm too tired today. 
I'll do it tomorrow." But when someone else is waiting for me, I have a little more motivation to show up. 
So playing sports with other people gives me both physical exercise and a chance to socialize.

→ How I started playing this sport

I started playing badminton quite casually. 
It wasn't something I planned to become seriously interested in from the beginning. 
I first tried it because it was a sport that people around me were already playing, and it didn't require an extremely complicated setup. 
I could simply get a racket, find a court, and start playing. 
At first, I wasn't very good at it. 
I had trouble controlling the shuttlecock, my timing was inconsistent, and I got tired quite quickly. 
However, I gradually became more comfortable with the basic movements. 
Once I started improving, the sport became much more enjoyable. 
I think that's an important part of getting into any new activity. 
In the beginning, you may feel awkward because you don't know what you're doing. 
But if you keep practicing, things slowly become more natural. 
After playing badminton several times, I realized that I actually enjoyed both the physical activity and the social side of the sport. 
That's how a casual activity eventually became one of my favorite sports.

→ Why I enjoy playing it

There are several reasons why I enjoy badminton. 
First, it's a very active sport. 
You have to move around constantly, react quickly, and pay attention to where the shuttlecock is going. 
So even though a game can be fun and relaxed, you can still get quite a good workout from it. 
Another reason is that badminton is challenging without being too intimidating. 
I can play casually, but if I want to improve, there are always new things to work on, such as footwork, timing, accuracy, and different types of shots. 
I also enjoy the social aspect. 
Playing with friends or colleagues gives us an opportunity to spend time together outside of work. 
We can talk, joke around, and enjoy some friendly competition. 
Most importantly, badminton helps me get away from my computer. 
Since I spend a lot of time sitting and working with technology, I really appreciate having an activity that makes me move. 
For me, it's the combination of exercise, challenge, and social interaction that makes badminton so enjoyable.

→ Physical benefits of regular exercise

Regular exercise has a lot of physical benefits, especially for people who don't move around much during the day. 
One obvious benefit is that it helps improve overall fitness. 
When you exercise consistently, your stamina can gradually improve, and everyday activities may become easier. 
Exercise can also help strengthen your muscles and improve your flexibility and coordination. 
Another important benefit is that it can help people maintain a healthy body weight when combined with a sensible diet. 
I also notice that I generally feel more energetic when I'm physically active on a regular basis. 
If I spend several days doing nothing but sitting and working, I tend to feel sluggish. 
Exercise gives me the opposite feeling. 
It wakes me up and makes my body feel more active. 
Of course, I don't think exercise is about trying to become extremely fit or having a perfect body. 
For me, the bigger goal is simply to maintain good physical condition and reduce the negative effects of a sedentary lifestyle. 
Even moderate exercise can make a meaningful difference if you do it consistently.

→ Mental benefits of playing sports

I think the mental benefits of sports are just as important as the physical ones. 
One of the biggest benefits for me is stress relief. 
When I'm playing badminton, I have to concentrate on the game, so I can't spend the whole time thinking about work or other problems. 
It gives my mind a temporary break. 
Exercise can also improve my mood. 
Even if I don't feel particularly motivated before a workout, I often feel better afterward. 
Another benefit is that sports can teach you how to deal with failure and frustration. 
You won't win every game, and you won't perform perfectly every time. 
Sometimes you'll make a stupid mistake or lose a point that you should have won. 
Instead of getting too frustrated, you have to move on and focus on the next point. 
I think that's actually a useful life skill. 
Sports can also give you a sense of achievement. 
When you notice that you're getting stronger, faster, or more skillful, you feel that your effort is producing results. 
So for me, sports are not just exercise for the body; they're also a way to refresh and train the mind.

→ Social benefits of playing sports

One thing I really like about sports is that they can bring people together. 
When you play a sport with other people, you naturally have opportunities to communicate, cooperate, and build relationships. 
This is especially true when you play regularly with the same group. 
You start getting to know people's personalities, their playing styles, and even their sense of humor. 
I think sports can also make socializing easier because you don't have to sit down and come up with a topic to talk about. 
The activity itself gives you something to share. 
For example, when I'm playing badminton with colleagues, we might talk about work before the game, joke around during the game, and then grab something to eat afterward. 
It feels much more natural than simply trying to make conversation. 
Sports can also teach teamwork and communication, especially team sports where everyone has a role to play. 
Even in an individual sport like badminton, you still learn how to communicate with your partner if you're playing doubles. 
So I think sports can improve not only your physical health but also your relationships with other people.

→ My most memorable sports experience

One of my most memorable sports experiences was a particularly competitive badminton session with friends and colleagues. 
Normally, we just play for fun, but on that occasion, everyone was surprisingly competitive. 
We had several close games where the score kept going back and forth, and nobody wanted to give up. 
I remember one game where we were very close to the end, and every single point felt important. 
Even though it wasn't an official competition, everyone was completely focused. 
What made the experience memorable was the atmosphere. 
We were laughing and joking between games, but as soon as the game started, everyone became serious again. 
Eventually, we finished the session completely exhausted. 
Looking back, I don't even remember who won every game, and honestly, that's not the important part. 
What I remember is how much fun we had and how energetic everyone felt afterward. 
Experiences like that are one of the main reasons I enjoy playing sports. 
They create memories that you probably wouldn't get from simply staying at home and watching TV.

→ The hardest sport I have tried

The hardest type of exercise I've tried is probably a very intense bodyweight workout, especially exercises like burpees or mountain climbers. 
They may look simple because you don't need any special equipment, but they're surprisingly exhausting when you do them continuously. 
The first time I tried a high-intensity workout, I underestimated how difficult it would be. 
After only a few minutes, my heart was beating extremely fast and I was already struggling to maintain the same pace. 
What makes this kind of exercise difficult is that it doesn't give you much time to recover. 
You have to keep moving, and your whole body is involved. 
Compared with badminton, where the intensity naturally goes up and down, a high-intensity workout can feel much more brutal because you're deliberately pushing yourself. 
At the same time, that's also what makes it satisfying. 
When you finish a difficult workout, you know that you challenged yourself. 
It has taught me that something doesn't have to look complicated to be physically demanding. 
Sometimes the simplest exercises can be the hardest ones.

→ Individual sports versus team sports

I think both individual sports and team sports have their own advantages. 
Individual sports give you more control over your own performance. 
If you make a mistake, you can't blame anyone else, so you have to take responsibility and think about how to improve. 
They can be good for people who enjoy independence and personal challenges. 
Team sports are different because success depends on how well everyone works together. 
You have to communicate, trust your teammates, and sometimes put the team's needs before your own. 
I think team sports can be particularly good for developing cooperation and communication skills. 
Personally, I enjoy both, but I probably prefer sports that allow me to compete while still having some social interaction. 
Badminton is a good example because I can play singles when I want an individual challenge or doubles when I want to work with a partner. 
So I don't think one type is necessarily better than the other. 
It really depends on your personality and what you're looking for. 
Some people want to challenge themselves individually, while others enjoy the energy and teamwork of being part of a group.

→ Sports I watched but never played

There are several sports that I enjoy watching even though I've never actually played them. 
Football is probably the best example. 
I think football is fascinating to watch because it's not just about individual skills. 
You also have tactics, teamwork, positioning, and quick decision-making. 
A team can completely change the game with one good play, which makes it exciting. 
Another sport I find interesting is basketball. 
The pace is very fast, and the game can change dramatically in just a few minutes. 
Sometimes I watch sports simply because I enjoy the atmosphere rather than because I understand every technical detail. 
Major sporting events can also be exciting because millions of people are watching and supporting their favorite teams at the same time. 
However, watching a sport and actually playing it are two very different experiences. 
When you're watching, you can appreciate the skill and strategy from the outside. 
When you're playing, you suddenly realize how difficult those professional athletes make everything look. 
That's one reason why watching sports can actually make me appreciate the athletes' abilities even more.

→ Sports I want to try someday

There are several sports I'd like to try someday, especially activities that are quite different from what I'm used to. 
One example is swimming. 
I think swimming would be a great full-body workout, and it's also a useful life skill. 
I'd also like to try cycling over a longer distance. 
I've ridden a bicycle casually before, but I've never really done a long cycling trip where the journey itself is the main goal. 
Another activity I'd be interested in trying is hiking. 
I like the idea of combining physical exercise with being outdoors and exploring new places. 
The main reason I haven't tried these activities yet is simply a lack of time and sometimes a lack of opportunity. 
I don't think I need to become good at every sport I try. 
For me, trying something new is more about having a different experience and finding out whether I enjoy it. 
If I have the opportunity in the future, I'd definitely like to step outside my comfort zone and experiment with some new sports.

→ How sports fit into my lifestyle

Sports are an important part of my lifestyle because my job is quite sedentary. 
I spend a large part of my working day sitting at a desk and looking at a computer screen, so I need some kind of physical activity to balance that out. 
I don't try to make sports the center of my life, but I do try to make them a regular part of my weekly routine. 
For example, I might play badminton on the weekend and do some shorter exercises during the week. 
I also try to take advantage of small opportunities to move instead of sitting continuously for hours. 
What I like about this approach is that it feels realistic. 
I don't need to spend two or three hours at the gym every day to consider myself active. 
I just need to make sure that exercise has a place in my routine. 
I think this is especially important as I get older. 
It's easy to focus on work and other responsibilities and tell yourself that you'll exercise later. 
But if you make physical activity a normal part of your lifestyle, it becomes much easier to maintain over the long term.

→ Why exercise matters for office workers

I think exercise is particularly important for office workers because many office jobs involve sitting for long periods of time. 
For someone who works at a computer, it's possible to spend most of the day sitting at a desk, staring at a screen, and barely moving. 
If that becomes a long-term habit, it can have a negative effect on your overall health and physical condition. 
That's why I think office workers need to make a conscious effort to stay active. 
Exercise can help counterbalance some of the physical effects of sitting all day. 
It can also be a great way to deal with work-related stress. 
When you're working on a difficult problem for several hours, your brain can become exhausted. 
Taking a break to walk, stretch, or exercise can help you reset. 
Another important point is that exercise doesn't necessarily mean going to the gym for an hour every day. 
You can stand up regularly, walk around during breaks, take the stairs, stretch at your desk, or do a short workout at home. 
For office workers, I think the most important thing is simply to avoid living a completely sedentary lifestyle. 
A healthy career is important, but maintaining your health while building that career is even more important.

EVERYDAY ACTIVITIES

→ My typical weekday morning routine

On a typical weekday, I usually get up fairly early because I need to get ready for work and commute to the office. 
I don't like rushing in the morning, so I try to give myself enough time to wake up properly and get everything ready. 
After getting out of bed, I usually wash my face, brush my teeth, and get dressed. 
I then have a simple breakfast because I don't usually have a lot of time in the morning. 
Before leaving, I make sure I have everything I need for work, such as my phone, wallet, keys, and other personal items. 
I also check the time so I know whether I'm running ahead or behind schedule. 
One thing I really enjoy during my morning routine is listening to English while I'm getting ready or during my commute. 
It allows me to practice English without having to create a separate block of time for it. 
Overall, my weekday mornings are fairly structured. 
I don't do anything particularly exciting, but having a predictable routine helps me start the day in a calm and organized way.

→ What I do before leaving home

Before leaving home, I usually go through a fairly simple checklist to make sure I haven't forgotten anything important. 
First, I get dressed and have breakfast. 
Then I prepare the things I'm going to take with me, such as my phone, wallet, keys, and anything I need for work. 
I usually take a quick look around the apartment as well. 
I make sure unnecessary lights are turned off, appliances are switched off, and everything is generally in order. 
I don't like coming home after work and finding out that I've forgotten something or left something running. 
I also check the time before I leave because my commute takes a certain amount of time, and I don't want to arrive late at work. 
If I have a little extra time, I might listen to some English content before heading out. 
I prefer having a few minutes of spare time rather than leaving at the last possible moment. 
Once I've checked everything, I lock the door and head to work. 
It's a simple routine, but doing the same things every morning makes them almost automatic.

→ How I usually commute to work

I usually commute to work by motorbike because it's relatively convenient for getting around the city. 
My commute takes roughly forty minutes in the morning, although the actual time can vary depending on traffic. 
Traffic in Ho Chi Minh City can be quite unpredictable, especially during rush hour, so I usually leave home early enough to give myself some extra time. 
One thing I like about my commute is that I can use it as an opportunity to listen to English. 
I often listen to podcasts, English conversations, or other learning materials while I'm traveling. 
Of course, I have to make sure that whatever I'm listening to doesn't distract me from the road. 
I don't try to actively study or repeat sentences while riding. 
I simply listen and let the content play in the background. 
On the way home, the journey can take a little longer because traffic is usually heavier. 
By that point, I'm often tired, so I prefer listening to something relaxing or entertaining. 
Although commuting can sometimes be frustrating, I've learned to make the most of that time instead of thinking of it as completely wasted time.

→ What my workday normally looks like

My normal workday is a mixture of development work, communication, meetings, and problem-solving. 
I usually start by checking what I need to work on and organizing my priorities for the day. 
Since I'm a frontend developer, a lot of my time is spent working with code, reviewing existing implementations, fixing bugs, or developing new features. 
Depending on the project, I might work with technologies such as React, Next.js, TypeScript, HTML, CSS, and other frontend tools. 
I also need to communicate with other developers, designers, or team members to clarify requirements and discuss technical issues. 
Some days are relatively smooth, where I can focus on one task for several hours. 
Other days are much more fragmented because I have to switch between different tasks or deal with unexpected problems. 
I usually try to make steady progress rather than trying to finish everything at once. 
By the end of the day, I normally review what I've done and think about what still needs to be completed. 
So although every day is slightly different, there is usually a familiar structure to my workday.

→ The most challenging part of work

The most challenging part of my work is probably dealing with problems that don't have an obvious solution. 
When I'm working on a normal feature, I can usually follow the requirements, break the task into smaller pieces, and implement it step by step. 
Bugs and unexpected issues are different. 
Sometimes the problem looks very simple from the outside, but after investigating it, I realize that several different parts of the system are connected to it. 
In those situations, I have to reproduce the problem, check the existing code, look at the data flow, and gradually narrow down the possible causes. 
Another challenge is that sometimes I have to work with code that I didn't originally write. 
It can take time to understand why something was implemented in a particular way. 
However, I think these difficult situations are also some of the best learning opportunities. 
When I finally understand the root cause and fix the problem, I usually remember the experience for a long time. 
So although difficult tasks can be frustrating, they also help me become a better developer.

→ What I usually do during lunch

During lunch, my main goal is to take a proper break from work. 
I usually have lunch with colleagues, and we often talk about things that aren't necessarily related to work. 
We might discuss what happened during the morning, plans for the weekend, food, movies, games, or just random things. 
I think having casual conversations like that is useful because it gives everyone a chance to relax and socialize. 
I don't like spending the entire lunch break thinking about work. 
After eating, I usually take some time to sit back and rest before going back to my desk. 
If I have enough time, I might check my phone or read something casually. 
I try not to start a difficult work task immediately after lunch because I know I need a little time to get my focus back. 
Lunch is also a good opportunity to mentally separate the first half of the day from the second half. 
Even though the break isn't very long, I usually feel more refreshed when I return to work afterward.

→ How I spend my afternoon

My afternoons are usually quite similar to my mornings in terms of work, but I sometimes feel that my energy level starts to drop as the day goes on. 
After lunch, I normally continue working on whatever tasks I have planned for the day. 
This might involve coding, debugging, reviewing code, or communicating with other team members. 
If I have a particularly difficult task, I try to work on it when I still have enough concentration rather than leaving everything until the end of the day. 
As the afternoon progresses, I sometimes take short breaks to stand up, stretch, get some water, or simply look away from the screen for a few minutes. 
I think these small breaks are important because staring at a computer continuously can be mentally and physically tiring. 
Later in the afternoon, I usually start thinking about what I've accomplished and what still needs to be done. 
If something is unfinished, I decide whether I should continue working on it or leave it for the next day. 
I try to avoid creating unnecessary pressure for myself at the end of the workday.

→ What I do after finishing work

After finishing work, I usually commute straight home. 
By that point, I've already spent most of the day working, so I normally want to relax a little before doing anything else. 
When I get home, I usually change into more comfortable clothes, wash up, and have dinner. 
I don't like immediately jumping from work into another demanding activity because I need some time to mentally switch off. 
After dinner, I decide what I want to do depending on how I feel that day. 
If I'm energetic, I might exercise, practice English, or spend some time on one of my hobbies. 
If I'm tired, I might simply watch something, listen to something, or play a game for a while. 
I think it's important to pay attention to my energy level instead of forcing myself to be productive every single evening. 
Some days are naturally more productive than others. 
The main thing for me is to create a clear boundary between work and personal life. 
Once I leave the office, I want to gradually shift my attention away from work and focus on my own time.

→ How I usually spend my evening

My evening usually depends on how tired I am after work. 
After dinner, I normally have some personal time that I can use however I want. 
On some evenings, I spend time practicing English because improving my speaking and listening skills is an important personal goal for me. 
I might listen to a podcast, review some useful expressions, or practice talking about a particular topic. 
On other evenings, I prefer doing something purely for entertainment. 
I might play a game, watch a show, or simply browse the internet for a while. 
If I have enough energy, I may also do some exercise. 
I don't necessarily follow exactly the same schedule every evening because I don't want my routine to feel too rigid. 
I think it's useful to have some flexibility. 
There are days when I want to be productive, and there are days when I just need to relax. 
As long as I don't spend the entire evening doing nothing but scrolling on my phone, I feel that I've had a reasonably good evening. 
My main goal is to enjoy my personal time while still taking care of the things that matter to me.

→ How I make time for hobbies

Finding time for hobbies can be difficult when you have a full-time job and other responsibilities, so I try to integrate my hobbies into my existing routine rather than waiting for a completely free day. 
For example, I use my commuting time to listen to English. 
That means I can practice listening without having to sacrifice my evening time. 
For physical activities like badminton, I usually put them on my weekend schedule because I have more free time then. 
During the week, I might do shorter activities that only take thirty minutes or so. 
I also try to avoid spending too much time on things that aren't particularly important to me. 
It's very easy to lose an hour scrolling through social media without even realizing it. 
If I reduce that kind of wasted time, I have more time for activities that I actually enjoy. 
I think making time for hobbies is less about having lots of free time and more about deciding what deserves your time. 
Even when I'm busy, I try to protect at least some personal time because I don't want my entire life to revolve around work.

→ How much English I practice daily

I practice English almost every day, although the type of practice varies depending on how much free time I have. 
I spend a significant amount of time listening to English, especially during my commute. 
Instead of treating commuting as completely wasted time, I use it to listen to podcasts, conversations, or other English content. 
In the evening, I usually spend some additional time practicing speaking. 
Sometimes I prepare a topic and try to talk about it for several minutes without reading a script. 
Other times, I review useful expressions and then try to use them in my own sentences. 
I don't expect myself to study grammar or vocabulary intensively every single day. 
My main focus is becoming more comfortable with understanding and producing natural spoken English. 
I think consistency is more important than studying for a huge number of hours once in a while. 
Even on a busy day, I can usually find some time to listen. 
Over time, these small daily sessions add up, and I've noticed that English has gradually become a more natural part of my everyday life.

→ What I usually do before bed

Before going to bed, I usually try to slow down and move away from work-related activities. 
I might finish whatever I'm doing on my computer, put my phone aside for a while, and prepare myself for sleep. 
I usually brush my teeth, wash up, and make sure everything is ready for the next morning. 
Sometimes I spend a little time thinking about what I need to do the following day. 
I find that mentally organizing the next day can make it easier to relax because I don't have to keep thinking about unfinished tasks while I'm trying to sleep. 
I also try not to do anything too mentally demanding right before bed. 
For example, if I have been studying or working for a long time, I prefer to switch to something lighter. 
I think having a consistent pre-bed routine is useful because it gives your brain a signal that the day is coming to an end. 
I don't always follow my routine perfectly, of course, but I try to keep the last part of my evening relatively calm rather than filling it with stimulating activities.

→ My typical bedtime and sleep routine

I try to maintain a fairly regular sleeping schedule because I notice a big difference in how I feel when I get enough sleep. 
On workdays, I usually try to go to bed at a reasonable time so that I can wake up without feeling completely exhausted the next morning. 
My evening routine gradually becomes quieter as bedtime approaches. 
I finish my personal activities, take care of basic things like brushing my teeth, and then try to relax. 
I don't always fall asleep immediately, especially if I've had a particularly busy or stressful day, but having a consistent routine helps. 
I also think the quality of sleep matters just as much as the number of hours. 
If I stay up very late using my phone or computer, I may wake up feeling tired even if I technically spend enough time in bed. 
That's why I try to avoid turning every evening into a late-night session. 
On weekends, I have a little more flexibility, but I still try not to completely destroy my normal sleep schedule. 
For me, good sleep is essential because it affects my energy, concentration, mood, and productivity the following day.

→ How my weekends are different

My weekends are quite different from my weekdays because I don't have to follow my normal work schedule. 
During the week, most of my time is structured around work, commuting, and other responsibilities. 
On the weekend, I have much more freedom to decide how I want to spend my time. 
I can wake up without immediately thinking about getting to the office, which already makes the morning feel more relaxed. 
I usually have more time for exercise, hobbies, English practice, and spending time with other people. 
At the same time, I don't want to make my weekends too busy. 
If I schedule too many activities, the weekend can start feeling like another working week. 
So I usually try to find a balance between being active and simply resting. 
One thing I particularly enjoy is having longer periods of uninterrupted time. 
During the week, I might only have thirty minutes for a hobby, but on the weekend I can spend several hours doing something without constantly checking the time. 
That's probably the biggest difference: weekdays are mostly about responsibilities, while weekends give me more control over my time.

→ How I usually spend Saturdays

Saturday is usually the day when I try to be more active. 
Since I don't have to go to work, I can start the day more slowly, but I also like to get some exercise in the morning. 
Badminton is one of the activities I enjoy doing on Saturdays because it gives me a chance to exercise and spend time with friends or colleagues. 
After exercising, I might have breakfast or coffee and then head home. 
The rest of the day is usually quite flexible. 
I might work on my English, play games, watch something, go out, or simply relax at home. 
I also use Saturday to take care of things that I don't have enough time for during the workweek. 
For example, I might clean the apartment, organize things, or deal with small personal tasks. 
I don't try to make every hour productive, though. 
I think Saturday should also include some downtime. 
If I've had a particularly tiring week, I might spend part of the afternoon doing absolutely nothing. 
For me, a good Saturday has a combination of exercise, personal interests, social time, and relaxation.

→ How I usually spend Sundays

I tend to have a quieter Sunday compared with Saturday. 
Since Sunday is the last day before another working week begins, I usually want to use it to relax and recharge. 
I might spend some time at home, watch something, play a game, listen to English, or work on personal interests. 
I also like having some unplanned time because I don't want every part of the weekend to be scheduled. 
At some point during the day, I usually start thinking about the upcoming week. 
I might check my schedule, think about what I need to do at work, and mentally prepare myself for Monday. 
I don't want to spend the whole day worrying about work, but a little preparation can make Monday morning feel less stressful. 
Sunday is also a good opportunity to catch up on things I didn't finish during the week. 
Overall, I think of Sunday as a transition day. 
It's still part of the weekend, so I want to enjoy it, but it's also a chance to slow down, organize myself, and get ready for another week.

→ The busiest part of my day

The busiest part of my day is usually the period between the beginning of work and lunchtime. 
That's when I tend to have the most energy and concentration, so I try to use that time for tasks that require more mental effort. 
If I have an important feature to implement or a difficult bug to investigate, I prefer to work on it in the morning rather than waiting until the afternoon. 
The morning can also become busy because there may be messages, meetings, or questions from other team members that I need to deal with. 
Sometimes several things happen at the same time, and I have to decide what is actually urgent and what can wait. 
I think one of the most important skills in a busy work environment is being able to prioritize. 
If I try to respond to everything immediately, I can easily lose focus. 
So I usually try to finish or make meaningful progress on one important task before switching to something else. 
Once lunchtime arrives, I feel that I've already completed the most mentally demanding part of the day.

→ The most relaxing part of my day

The most relaxing part of my day is usually the evening after I've finished dinner and don't have any urgent responsibilities left. 
At that point, I know that the working day is over, so I can finally stop thinking about deadlines, meetings, bugs, and other work-related problems. 
I might listen to something, watch a show, play a game, practice English, or simply sit down and relax. 
What makes this time particularly enjoyable is that I don't have to be productive all the time. 
During the day, there are always things that need to be done, but in the evening I have more control over how I spend my time. 
Sometimes the most relaxing thing is actually doing something very simple. 
I don't necessarily need to go somewhere or do anything exciting. 
Just being at home in a comfortable environment can be enough. 
I think having this kind of quiet personal time is important because it helps me mentally disconnect from work. 
Without that separation, it would be easy to feel like the entire day is just one long working session.

→ Things I want to change

Even though I'm reasonably happy with my current routine, there are still several things I'd like to improve. 
One thing is that I sometimes spend more time than I should on my phone or on other forms of passive entertainment. 
It's very easy to say, "I'll just check my phone for five minutes," and suddenly realize that thirty minutes have passed. 
I'd like to become more intentional about how I use that time. 
I also want to be more consistent with exercise. 
I already do some physical activities, but there are weeks when my schedule gets busy and I don't exercise as much as I planned. 
Another thing I'd like to improve is my sleep routine. 
I think going to bed at a consistent time would make my mornings easier and give me more energy during the day. 
Finally, I'd like to spend more time doing meaningful activities rather than simply filling my free time. 
That doesn't mean I want every minute of my day to be productive. 
I just want to have a better balance between work, exercise, learning, hobbies, socializing, and proper rest.

→ My ideal daily routine

My ideal daily routine would be structured enough to keep me organized but flexible enough that I wouldn't feel trapped by a strict schedule. 
I would like to start the morning without rushing. 
After getting ready and having a healthy breakfast, I would spend some time doing light exercise or simply preparing mentally for the day. 
During work, I would focus on the most important tasks when my energy and concentration are at their highest. 
I would also take regular short breaks instead of sitting in front of a screen continuously. 
After work, I'd like to have a clear transition between my professional and personal life. 
I would have dinner, spend some time with people I care about, and then choose an activity depending on my energy level. 
Some days I might exercise, while other days I might practice English or enjoy one of my hobbies. 
In the evening, I'd gradually slow down and avoid staying up too late. 
Ideally, I'd get enough sleep and wake up feeling refreshed the next morning. 
For me, the perfect routine isn't about being productive every minute. 
It's about having enough time for work, health, learning, relationships, hobbies, and rest without constantly feeling rushed.
